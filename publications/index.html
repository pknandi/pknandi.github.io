  1. ECCV
    BaFCo: A Document Understanding Benchmark for Complex Bangla Form Comprehension
    Abu Tyeb Azad, Ishita Sur Apan, Fahim Ahmed, Sumaiya Karim Katha, Ezharuddin Jubaer, Armun Alam, Pranjal Kumar Nandi, Amin Ahsan Ali, Aman Chadha, Md Mofijul Islam, and AKM Mahbubur Rahman
    In European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), Malmö, Sweden, 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML PDF Poster

    BaFCo is a benchmark of 200 multi-page Bangladeshi government forms spanning sectors such as agriculture, education, banking, and land management, annotated with 26 fine-grained entity types for Document Layout Analysis and Key Information Extraction. We evaluate frontier and open multimodal LLMs under zero-shot and chain-of-thought prompting and find that current models struggle to comprehend Bangla forms, especially when localizing fine-grained entities.

2024

  1. JNCA
    Task offloading to edge cloud balancing utility and cost for energy harvesting Internet of Things
    Pranjal Kumar Nandi, Md Rejaul Islam Reaj, Sujan Sarker, Md Abdur Razzaque, Md Mamun-or-Rashid, and Palash Roy
    Journal of Network and Computer Applications, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    We propose a task offloading policy for energy-harvesting Internet of Things devices that balances device utility, modeled from task latency and energy consumption, against the cost of executing tasks on the edge cloud, formulating offloading as a subset selection problem.

  1. Patent App.
    Using Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning for Remote Monitoring and Assisting of Individuals Under Care, and Systems and Methods Therefor
    James Michael Kelly, David Lawrence Turock, Justin Mark Brockie, Md Asif Ali, Meheraj Hossain, Raheeb Hassan, Md Nazrul Islam, Rafid Ameer Mahmud, Pranjal Kumar Nandi, Richard Allen Robbins, William Edward Sepesi, Md Rayed Bin Wahed, and James Joseph Brockman
    Aug 2026
    US Patent Application Publication US20260229324A1 (pending). Assignee: Therap Services LLC.
    Abs Bib HTML

    A HIPAA-compliant system for remotely monitoring and assisting individuals under care. Sensor data from the individual’s location is converted into textual descriptions, combined into a unified description, and matched against a database of known events; the matching event’s action plan, such as alerting a caregiver whose caseload permits access to the individual’s information, is then carried out. Unmatched events can be labeled with a new action, which is stored for future use.
